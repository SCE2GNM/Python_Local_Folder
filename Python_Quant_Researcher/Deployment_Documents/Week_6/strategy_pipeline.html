STEP 0.1
Academic Literature Search
SSRN, arXiv quant-fin, Journal of Portfolio Management. Peer-reviewed papers on indicator performance in crypto. Recent papers (2-3 years) often contain unexploited insights.
Why academic first?Peer-reviewed research has been through scrutiny and uses proper out-of-sample methodology. Citation count ≠ best strategy — highly cited papers are often cited for methodology contributions, not alpha generation. Search for recent papers citing foundational papers to find novel extensions.
STEP 0.2
Practitioner Research
QuantConnect research library, Quantpedia strategy database, Ernest Chan blog, Marcos Lopez de Prado papers. Real backtested implementations with code.
Key practitioner sources:QuantConnect: open strategy library with actual code. Quantpedia: curated strategy database with source papers. Lopez de Prado: current professional quant standard. Ernest Chan: retail-scale algorithmic trading focus. Avoid: Blog posts without methodology, YouTube tutorials, any source not showing out-of-sample results.
STEP 0.3
Crypto-Specific Sources
Glassnode research, The Block Research. Crypto has different microstructure to equities — traditional equity findings often don't transfer cleanly.
Why crypto-specific matters:Crypto is 24/7 (no overnight gaps in traditional sense), higher volatility, different liquidity profile, regime-switching between bull/bear cycles. Parameters that work for equities often fail on crypto. ETH-optimal ADX parameters ranked 389th when applied to BTC — assets require separate optimisation.
STEP 0.4
Produce Research Brief
Save WEEK_N_RESEARCH_BRIEF.md. Answers 5 questions per strategy: best parameter ranges, entry/exit conditions, regime filters, known failure modes, crypto-specific findings.
5 questions the brief must answer:1. What parameter ranges have strongest empirical support? 2. What entry/exit conditions are supported? 3. What regime filters improve performance? 4. What are the known failure modes? 5. Has this been tested on crypto specifically? Output guides the grid search bounds in Phase 3 — preventing arbitrary parameter ranges.

STEP 1.1
Explain Economic Logic
Why should this strategy work? What market inefficiency does it exploit? Trend-following exploits momentum persistence. Mean reversion exploits overshooting. No logic = no edge.
Economic logic test:If you can't explain in one sentence why this strategy should generate returns above buy-and-hold, don't build it. Example: "ETH ADX enters when trend strength is confirmed — capturing sustained directional moves while avoiding choppy markets." Clear, testable, falsifiable.
STEP 1.2
Payoff Profile Sanity Check
Calculate breakeven win rate: p_breakeven = 1 / (1 + b) where b = avg_win / avg_loss. If expected live win rate is below breakeven, Kelly says don't trade.
⚠️ Critical check — ETH RSI example:b = 5.79% avg win / 15.00% avg loss = 0.386 p_breakeven = 1 / (1 + 0.386) = 72.1% Expected live win rate: 60-70% → BELOW breakeven. This single check would have caught the RSI asymmetric payoff problem at Phase 1 rather than at pre-deployment review. Rule: if b < 1 (losses larger than wins), flag immediately.
STEP 1.3
Identify Strategy Type & Regime
Trend-following (ADX, SMA, MACD) or mean reversion (RSI, BB)? Which market regime suits it? Trend strategies fail in ranging markets; mean reversion fails in trending markets.
Why regime matters for portfolio:ETH ADX (trend-following) and ETH RSI (mean reversion) are complementary — they signal in different market conditions. When ADX is FLAT (no trend), RSI may be active. This natural regime self-selection means strategies complement rather than compete. Design the portfolio for all-weather coverage.
STEP 1.4
Create Risk Register
Document every known risk before the first line of code. Capture assumptions about data quality, market conditions, execution. HIGH items flagged for mandatory resolution.
Risk Register structure:ID: unique reference (e.g. A016, RR-RSI-001) Severity: HIGH / MAJOR / MEDIUM / LOW Status: OPEN / RESOLVED Description: what could go wrong Mitigation: what we're doing about it Update log: timestamped changes HIGH = must resolve before deployment. MAJOR = resolve or formally accept with rationale. MEDIUM/LOW = document and monitor.
Logic verified → first backtest

STEP 2.1
Cost Model Applied
0.15% round-trip transaction costs. 0.10% stop slippage (realistic for small positions on liquid pairs). Bar-by-bar stop checking against daily LOW prices — not just closes.
Realistic cost model (Phases 2-4):Transaction costs: 0.15% round-trip Stop slippage: 0.10% below intended stop Liquidation slippage: 0.25% Interest: 0.015%/day on borrowed amount during open positions only Conservative stress model (Phase 5 only): Transaction costs: 0.30% round-trip Stop slippage: 0.25% Liquidation slippage: 0.50% Using stress costs in Phase 2 understates strategy quality. Using realistic costs in Phase 5 defeats the purpose of stress testing.
STEP 2.2
Correct Metric Calculations
Sortino and Sharpe from daily equity curve. Report BOTH per-trade MaxDD AND daily MtM MaxDD. Entry date at entry, not exit. These are mandatory — per-trade Sortino inflates 3-4×.
Daily equity curve method:Build a daily portfolio value series: during open position, equity = capital × (current_price / entry_price). Between trades, equity stays flat at last exit value. Sortino = mean(daily_returns) / std(daily_returns where ret < 0) × sqrt(365) Per-trade MaxDD: worst sequence of completed trade returns. Daily MtM MaxDD: worst portfolio value vs previous peak, every day — this is what you see watching a live account.
STEP 2.3
Maximum Loss Per Trade Check
Calculate: max_loss = position_size × stop_pct. Express as % of total allocation. If max_loss % is high AND win rate is below 80%, flag for review before proceeding.
Why this matters — RSI example:$500 position × 15% stop = $75 max loss = 15% of $500 allocation. If win rate degrades to 70% (expected live), 3 consecutive losses = −45% of allocation. Flag rule: if max_loss > 20% of allocation AND expected live win rate < 80%, mandatory review before Phase 3.
STEP 2.4
Sample Size Check
Minimum 30 trades required. Below 30: flag on every metric output. Below 100: Monte Carlo simulation mandatory in Phase 3. Statistical inference is unreliable at small sample sizes.
Sample size implications:ETH ADX: 158 trades → reliable statistics, Kelly sizing trustworthy. ETH RSI: 31 trades → 95% CI on win rate spans 79-99% (very wide). Cannot make confident deployment decisions. BTC SMA: 30 trades → borderline minimum. Walk-forward windows have 3-7 trades each — statistically unreliable. Monte Carlo mandatory below 100 trades because the backtest path is one of many possible outcomes.
✓ COSTS APPLIED
Gate: Costs AppliedAll backtest scripts must include 0.15% round-trip costs deducted from every trade. Previously missing from early backtests — caught in Week 5. Without costs, high-frequency strategies appear artificially profitable.
✓ DAILY LOW STOP CHECK
Gate: Bar-by-bar Low Stop CheckStop loss must be checked against the daily LOW price on every bar, not just the closing price. An intraday drop can trigger your stop even if the closing price is above the stop level. Confirmed correct in all Week 6 backtests — critical for accurate stop simulation.
✓ DAILY EQUITY SORTINO
Gate: Daily Equity Curve SortinoPer-trade method inflates Sortino by 3-4×. ETH ADX Sortino corrected from ~3.5 to 1.780 when this was fixed. Must explicitly verify in every new backtest script that the daily equity curve method is used, not per-trade returns.
✓ PAYOFF PROFILE CHECK
Gate: Payoff Profile Sanity Checkp_breakeven = 1/(1+b) where b = avg_win/avg_loss. If expected live win rate is below breakeven, strategy has negative Kelly expectation. This is the single check that would have prevented the ETH RSI $500 deployment — caught at Phase 2 it saves weeks of work.

STEP 3.1
Parameter Grid Search
MacBook runs all combinations (not Claude). Claude Code writes the script. Grid bounds come from research brief — not arbitrary. Report full leaderboard ranked by annual return%.
Who does the computation?Claude Code writes the Python script. Your MacBook executes it — all 728+ combinations. Claude only sees the output when done. Token cost is writing the script and reading results, not running the calculations. MacBook M3 8GB handles daily candle backtests easily through at least Week 12.
STEP 3.2
Grid Boundary Check
Best result must NOT sit at the edge of the tested parameter range. If the optimum is at the maximum or minimum of the grid, the true optimum is outside — extend the range and rerun.
Grid boundary example:If you test ADX threshold 15-25 and the best result is at threshold=25, the true optimum might be 28 or 30. You haven't found the peak — you've found the edge of your search space. Always extend and rerun when the winner sits at a boundary.
STEP 3.3
Cliff-Edge Check
Plot annual return% across the sensitivity curve for each parameter. The chosen parameters must sit at or near the PEAK — not on a declining slope toward a cliff. Fragile peaks are overfit.
Cliff-edge vs plateau:Good: broad plateau where ±20% parameter change has minimal impact. Strategy is robust. Bad: narrow peak where changing a parameter by 1 unit causes a sharp drop in performance. Strategy is overfit to the training data and will likely fail out-of-sample. A parameter sitting on a declining slope means you've optimised past the peak — performance will degrade in live trading.
STEP 3.4
Preliminary Leverage Screen
After identifying top 20 by 1× return, run coarse leverage screen (1.0×-3.0×, 0.5× steps) for each. Re-rank by leveraged return. The 1× winner is not always the leveraged winner.
Why leverage changes the ranking:A strategy with lower raw return but lower drawdown may support higher safe leverage, producing better final levered returns than a higher-raw-return strategy constrained by its drawdown. Example: Strategy A: 60% annual, -40% MDD → optimal leverage 1.5× → 90% effective return. Strategy B: 40% annual, -15% MDD → optimal leverage 3.0× → 120% effective return. Strategy B wins despite lower 1× performance. Low MaxDD and high Sortino are leverage multipliers — not just quality filters.
STEP 3.5
Monte Carlo (if n < 100)
1,000 simulations at 5 win rate scenarios: backtest WR, 80%, 75%, 70%, 65%. Shows full distribution of outcomes. Mandatory below 100 trades. Reveals win-rate sensitivity before deployment.
Monte Carlo output:For each win rate scenario: • Median annual return% • 10th percentile (bad luck scenario) • 90th percentile (good luck scenario) • Probability of negative year • Kelly fraction at this win rate ETH RSI Monte Carlo result at 70% WR: Median: −3.4%, Prob negative year: 74%, Kelly: −7.7% (negative!) This single analysis changed the deployment from $500 to $150 validation only.
STEP 3.6
Kelly Position Sizing
f* = (p×b − q) / b. Apply half-Kelly. Position = (f* × capital) / stop_pct. This ensures maximum loss = Kelly fraction of capital. NOT position = f* × capital (20× too small).
Kelly correct formula:Kelly f* is the RISK fraction, not the deployment fraction. WRONG: position = 12.41% × $1,000 = $124 (deployed $124, risk = $6.20) CORRECT: position = ($124.10) / 0.08 = $1,551 capped at capital (risk = $124.10 ✓) The wrong version was deployed from Week 4 to Week 6 — 20× undersizing. Maximum loss should equal Kelly fraction of capital, not Kelly fraction × stop%.

STRATEGIC_FRAMEWORK.mdThe decision-making philosophy. Critical guidance for Phase 4: cross-asset failure = asset-specific edge, not disqualification. STEP 4.1
Walk-Forward Validation
BOTH expanding window (anchored) AND rolling window (sliding). Strategy must be profitable in all windows. Document explicitly if both methods produce identical results (fixed parameters).
Two walk-forward methods:Expanding (anchored): training always starts at inception, grows each window. Tests whether strategy works as history accumulates. Rolling (sliding): fixed-length training window slides forward. Tests whether strategy works across different regimes. With fixed parameters (no per-window re-optimisation), both methods produce identical results — document this explicitly. True walk-forward with re-optimisation per window is the stronger test. ETH ADX: both methods confirmed ADX 20/10 (later revised to 19/9) as dominant.
STEP 4.2
Stability Analysis
Vary each parameter ±30% independently. Count % of variations that remain profitable. STABLE >60%, MARGINAL 40-60%, FRAGILE <40%. ETH ADX: MARGINAL at 58%. Acceptable.
Stability classification:STABLE (>60%): broad parameter plateau — strategy is robust. High confidence for deployment. MARGINAL (40-60%): acceptable with monitoring. Most deployed strategies fall here. FRAGILE (<40%): narrow peak — likely overfit. Do not deploy. ETH ADX: MARGINAL 58% — acceptable, deployed with awareness. BTC SMA: MARGINAL 50.5% — borderline. BTC ADX: MARGINAL 54.5% — acceptable. Stability reduces confidence in Kelly sizing estimates — consider more conservative sizing for MARGINAL strategies.
STEP 4.3
Cross-Asset Check
Run strategy on a different asset. Failure means asset-specific edge — not automatic disqualification. ETH ADX generalises to BTC. BTC SMA fails on ETH. Deploy on validated asset only.
Cross-asset failure interpretation:Failure means the edge is asset-specific — which is fine if the strategy is only deployed on its validated asset. ETH-optimal ADX parameters (period 9, threshold 19) ranked 389th when applied to BTC. BTC required its own optimisation (period 14, threshold 19). This teaches that ETH and BTC have structurally different trend characteristics. Never assume parameters transfer between assets.
STEP 4.4
B&H Relative Thresholds
All three must pass: Annual return ≥2.0× B&H, MaxDD ≤0.50× B&H MaxDD, Sortino ≥1.50× B&H Sortino. A strategy beating B&H on only one metric doesn't have a clear edge over passive holding.
B&H threshold results — ETH ADX:Annual: 80.1% vs 12.9% B&H = 6.2× ✅ (target ≥2.0×) MaxDD: 37.7% vs B&H MaxDD = 0.46× ✅ (target ≤0.50×) Sortino: 1.780 vs 0.786 B&H = 2.26× ✅ (target ≥1.5×) All three pass. BTC SMA Sortino: 1.43× — narrowly below 1.5× threshold. Accepted as marginal deviation given strong performance on other two metrics. Formal written rationale required for any threshold not met.

LAYER 1
Realistic Figures (Baseline)
Expected performance under normal conditions. Realistic costs: 0.15% round-trip, 0.10% stop slippage. This is the headline figure — what you expect in live trading.

LAYER 2
Conservative Costs
Double transaction costs (0.30% round-trip), higher slippage (0.25% stop, 0.50% liquidation). Strategy must remain profitable. Tests survivability under adverse execution.
Layer 2 — conservative stress:Tests: what if fills are consistently worse than expected? What if there's a period of high spread or reduced liquidity? The strategy must remain profitable and Sortino must stay above 1.0. If it fails Layer 2, the edge is too thin for the cost environment. Do not deploy.
LAYER 3
Parameter Degradation
Win rate -20%, avg win -20%, avg loss worsened -20%, all simultaneously (scenario d). If profitable: deploy. If scenario d fails but a/b/c pass: reduce initial capital to 50% pending live validation.
Layer 3 — parameter degradation scenarios:a) Win rate degrades 20% b) Average win degrades 20% c) Average loss worsens 20% d) All three simultaneously — the harshest case Scenario (d) simulates a future where market conditions have changed and the strategy's edge has partially eroded. If it still makes money under (d), deploy with confidence. If not, deploy at 50% capital pending 20 live trades confirming the edge persists.
LEVERAGE
Leverage Optimisation & Buffer
Grid 1.0×-5.0× in 0.1× steps. Interest cost modelling. Bar-by-bar liquidation check vs daily lows. Safety buffer ≥33%. Categorical liquidation test: worst historical drop must not liquidate from fresh entry.
Categorical liquidation test:Worst ETH single-day drop: −42.9% (March 12, 2020) Worst BTC single-day drop: −38.6% (same day) At recommended leverage, if this drop would liquidate the position even from a fresh entry — the leverage is categorically unsafe. Reduce leverage until the test passes. BTC SMA 2.5×: FAILS test (liquidated from fresh entry by −38.6% drop). BTC SMA 2.0×: PASSES (MR drops to 11%, above 5% liquidation threshold). ETH ADX 1.9×: PASSES from fresh entry (MR drops to 17%).

STEP 6.4

Score strategy on: sample size (primary), stability result, walk-forward consistency, B&H multiple. Higher confidence = larger allocation. Do not give equal capital to a 30-trade and a 158-trade strategy.

Live Performance Gates — ETH RSI
Active decision rules firing automatically from record_trade_result() data. Scale-up: 20 live trades + WR ≥80% → reserved_capital $150 → $341. Stop: WR <72% after 20 live trades → pause bot, reassess.
These are active rules — not future work. record_trade_result() appends every closed trade to data/eth_rsi_live_performance_log.csv and returns running stats (n_trades, win_rate, consecutive_losses). Scale-up trigger (both conditions required): n_trades ≥ 20 AND win_rate ≥ 0.80 → update portfolio_state.json: reserved_capital $150 → $341 → Telegram alert: "ETH RSI scale-up threshold reached — increasing capital" → Next trade sizes from $341 capital base Stop trigger (both conditions required): n_trades ≥ 20 AND win_rate < 0.72 → set DRY_RUN = True (pause live orders) → Telegram alert: "ETH RSI win rate below breakeven — bot paused, reassess" → Review trade log, check regime filter, consider shelving Why 72% is the hard floor: Breakeven win rate = 1 / (1 + b) where b = avg_win / avg_loss = 5.79% / 15.00% = 0.386 p_breakeven = 1 / 1.386 = 72.1% Below 72.1% the strategy has negative Kelly expectation — every trade destroys capital in expectation. This is not a soft warning; it is a mathematical stop condition.

🐍 portfolio_manager.py

7KB Live on EC2 All bots import this
Why portfolio_manager exists:Without it: Bot 1 deploys $1,000, USDT balance drops. Bot 2 reads live balance and thinks its 25% share is now $250 instead of $500 — wrong. With it: each bot calls get_my_capital('eth_adx') which returns reserved_capital = $1,000 from portfolio_state.json. Never reads live USDT balance for sizing. Weekly rebalance recalculates reserved_capital based on actual portfolio value — this is how gains compound.
